# Gender Equality


The study uses quantitative data from Eurostat, primarily from 2018 and 2019, and applies various statistical techniques such as stratified random sampling, hypothesis testing, ANOVA analysis, and regression analysis.


## Key findings from the empirical analysis are:

- **Stratified Random Sampling**: The data was segmented by geographical regions in Europe, with a sample size of 15 countries chosen from these regions. The regions were divided as follows: Northern Europe (4 countries), Western Europe (3), Central Europe (2), Southeast Europe (3), and Eastern Europe (3).

- **Hypothesis Testing**: The research tested the unemployment rate's relationship to a 5% threshold. It was concluded that while the unemployment rate is not significantly less than 5%, it differs significantly from 5% overall.

- **ANOVA Analysis**: Significant regional differences in unemployment rates were observed, confirming that the region significantly affects the unemployment rate.

- **Simple Linear Regression Analysis**: There was no statistically significant relationship between educational attainment and the gender earnings gap, suggesting that education alone does not explain a large portion of earnings disparities.

- **Multiple Linear Regression Analysis**: The interaction between education and EU membership had a statistically significant effect on the unemployment rate, although the direct effects of education and EU membership alone were not significant.


## Conclusion:

The study reveals that regional differences in unemployment rates persist in Europe, with educational attainment and EU membership influencing these rates. However, there was no conclusive evidence that education alone significantly narrows the gender earnings gap. The interaction between education and EU membership, though, appears to have a meaningful effect on unemployment rates.
